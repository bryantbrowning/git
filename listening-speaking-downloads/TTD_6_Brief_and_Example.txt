VOCABULARY EXAMPLES

SHORT DEMONSTRATION EXTRACT (not full submission length)
These textbook pages explain how to organize a cause–effect presentation. A cause helps bring about an effect. An effect is a result, and a habitat is a place supporting living things. First, notice the warning: one event happening after another is not enough to prove causation. Second, the Arctic example links warming with loss of sea ice used by animals for hunting. Third, a different pathway links melting land ice with rising sea levels. Does sequence alone prove a cause? No. Which pathway adds water to the ocean? Melting land ice. We should not join every impact into one straight chain. Some effects branch, and local risk depends on other conditions. My summary is to name the change, explain each arrow, distinguish branches and identify what needs more evidence. I would use dated local research before making a forecast for a particular place.

COMPREHENSION CHECKS
Does one event happening after another prove that it caused it?
Answer: No; a connecting mechanism/evidence is needed.
Which melting pathway adds water to the ocean: land ice or already-floating sea ice?
Answer: Land ice. Floating sea ice has different important effects, including habitat changes.

EVALUATION / SOURCE LIMIT
Keep the sea-ice/hunting and land-ice/sea-level pathways separate. Old regional projections in a textbook are not current forecasts; label inferences and seek dated evidence.
