TEACH THE DOCUMENT #5
From research notes to a mini-brief
Unit 4 · launch Lesson 1; due at Lesson 2's start.

FINAL: one 3–5-minute English recording; visible genuine document; brief cue notes, not full-script reading.
The instructor announces the actual calendar and submission route.

SOURCE SELECTION
A genuine English educational/research document about weather, climate or an affected environment; a credited coursebook paragraph is acceptable.

REQUIREMENTS
- Show a readable genuine source with title/publisher/page or URL; identify its date if available.
- Teach 3–5 words and 3–5 key ideas; select only information you can explain accurately.
- Separate observed information, source claims and predictions; do not invent current statistics.
- Use brief notes, show source evidence and ask at least two comprehension questions.
- Give a short summary, a source limitation and one question for further research.

WORKED SOURCE
Worked source: Western Ghats preparation text, textbook p.92

- The text locates the Western Ghats in western India.
- It describes rainforests and unusual wildlife.
- It discusses changing rainfall, plant growth and effects on the environment.

CREDIT
Unlock, second edition, Level 2 Listening and Speaking · Cambridge University Press · printed pages 92.

VOCABULARY EXAMPLES
- rainforest: a forest in a high-rainfall region
- wildlife: animals living in their natural environment
- research: careful study to answer a question

SHORT DEMONSTRATION EXTRACT (not full submission length)
This is the preparation paragraph on page 92 of our textbook. It introduces the Western Ghats before a listening report. A rainforest is a forest in a high-rainfall region. Wildlife means animals in the natural environment, and research is careful study of a question. The text locates the Western Ghats in western India. It describes unusual wildlife and discusses changing rainfall and plant growth. Where is the location? Western India. Does this paragraph give a new measurement from this year? No. It helps us predict that the report may discuss weather, wildlife and environmental effects. However, a current claim about rainfall would need dated regional research. My summary is that background reading can help a listener prepare, but it should not be treated as a live forecast.

EVALUATION / SOURCE LIMIT
The text gives background for predicting report topics. Current rainfall trends or numerical forecasts need dated regional research; do not present the source as live information.
